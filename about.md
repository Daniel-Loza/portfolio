# About Me

<img src="headshot.jpg" width=200px>

My name is Daniel Loza, and I am currently pursuing a Bachelor's in electrical engineering at the Georgia Institute of Technology. I first enrolled in the summer 2026 semester. Since being admitted, I have dedicated my time here to learning and enjoying as many practical engineering skills as possible. My current engineering interests are nanotechnology fabrication and chip design.

I was first introduced to semiconductor devices and nanofabrication during my first summer semester at Georgia Tech. The Nanotechnology and Photonics Society club had just been created. After joining a project and researching the process of lithography, I joined and proposed design steps for the club's first lithostepper. Since the new club needed administrative assistance, I then joined the operations team and took on the project lead role for lithography. We are currently working on funding and building our first lithostepper.

Additionally, I took interest in transistors and their fundamental role in high-level logic and chip design. To learn more, I joined the SiliconJackets. Through an onboarding project, I designed, verified, and physically designed a partial RISC-V processor. In the process, I learned about and built with Verilog hardware description, testbenches, Synopsys software, and Cadence software. I will continue to gain more expertise through my time on the physical design team.

Beyond engineering, I have also continued to dedicate my time to the cello. Having been classically trained for over a decade, I am also a member of the Georgia Tech Symphony Orchestra's cello section.
